# History

The open-source movement is rooted in decades of sharing source code, from the early hacker culture of university laboratories to the free-software ideals of the FSF and the pragmatism of the Open Source Initiative. Understanding this history explains why today's infrastructure is organised the way it is.

## Timeline of open source

### The prehistory: hacker culture (1950s-1970s)

- Software was shipped with hardware and source code was shared freely between researchers and hobbyists.
- The **ARPANET** and university computer labs created the culture of collaboration that later produced GNU and the Internet.

### Unix and the C language (1969-1970s)

- Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie create **Unix** at Bell Labs.
- Ritchie invents **C** (1972). Unix spreads through academia, and its source code is widely shared and studied - the direct ancestor of today's open systems.

### The GNU Project (1983-1991)

- **Richard Stallman** announces the GNU Project (September 1983), aiming to build a complete free Unix-like operating system.
- Stallman founds the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** in 1985 and publishes the **GNU General Public License (GPL)** in 1989 - the first strong copyleft licence.
- GNU tools (Emacs, gcc, bash, coreutils) become the standard open toolchain.

### The kernel: Linux (1991)

- **Linus Torvalds** releases the first Linux kernel (0.01) in 1991 on the comp.os.minix newsgroup.
- Combined with GNU tools, Linux forms a complete, free operating system - "GNU/Linux".
- BSD (Berkeley Software Distribution) also continues as a free Unix lineage (FreeBSD, OpenBSD, NetBSD).

### The term "open source" and the OSI (1998)

- Netscape releases Navigator's source code under a free licence (1998) - a turning point.
- **Eric Raymond, Bruce Perens** and others coin the term **"open source"** and co-found the **Open Source Initiative (OSI)**.
- The Debian Free Software Guidelines become the basis of the **Open Source Definition**.

### The dot-com boom and the rise of the web stack (1998-2005)

- **Apache** becomes the dominant web server; the **LAMP stack** (Linux, Apache, MySQL, PHP) powers much of the early web.
- **Mozilla** is born from Netscape; later Firefox regains browser market share.
- **SourceForge** (1999) popularises hosting; **Git** (2005) and **GitHub** (2008) transform collaboration.
- **Wikipedia** (2001) demonstrates open collaboration at scale.

### The modern era: clouds, containers, open standards (2006-present)

- **Android** (2008) makes the Linux kernel the world's most widely deployed OS (by devices).
- **Kubernetes** (2014) standardises container orchestration; **Docker** (2013) popularises containers.
- Open clouds (**OpenStack**, **OpenShift**), infrastructure as code (**Terraform**, **Ansible**) and open data standards dominate enterprise tech.
- Open-source licences and business models mature: Red Hat's acquisition, the rise of **open-source companies** such as GitLab, HashiCorp, Confluent.
- The debate over licence "freedom vs sustainability" continues, with moves like **OpenTofu** (fork of Terraform) reflecting community pushback on licence changes.

## Key turning points

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1969 | Unix created at Bell Labs |
| 1972 | C language created |
| 1983 | GNU Project announced by Stallman |
| 1985 | Free Software Foundation founded |
| 1989 | First GPL licence published |
| 1991 | Linux kernel released by Torvalds |
| 1998 | Netscape open-sources Navigator; OSI founded; term "open source" coined |
| 2001 | Wikipedia launched |
| 2005 | Git created by Torvalds |
| 2008 | GitHub launched; Android released |
| 2013 | Docker released |
| 2014 | Kubernetes released by Google (donated to CNCF in 2015) |

## Documentary films

A visual introduction to the history of free software and open source. This is the author's playlist of documentaries about the Internet, Netscape, Linux, BSD and open-source culture.

1. **Project Code Rush - The Beginnings of Netscape/Mozilla** — a documentary following the Netscape engineers as they prepare to release the browser's source code, which became the seed of Mozilla and Firefox. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Q7FTjhvZ7Y)
2. **Default to Open: The Story of Open Source and Red Hat** — a Red Hat Films documentary about the origins and growth of open source through the 1980s and 1990s. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vhYMRtqvMg8)
3. **Nerds 2.0.1 - A Brief History of the Internet, Part 3: Wiring the World** — the story of how the Internet was physically built, from cables to routers. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b3bxCCI-pV0)
4. **Nerds 2.0.1 - A Brief History of the Internet, Volume I: Networking the Nerds** — the origins of the ARPANET and the first networkers. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4D2nxQBmOM)
5. **A Brief History of the Internet, Part 2: Serving the Suits** — how business and government shaped the early Internet. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxXTx0y5JI0)
6. **Kirk McKusick - A Narrative History of BSD** — the history of the Berkeley Software Distribution told by one of its leading developers. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DEEr6dT-4uQ)
7. **The Mother of All Demos, presented by Douglas Engelbart (1968)** — the legendary 1968 demo of the computer mouse, hypertext and collaborative computing. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJDv-zdhzMY)
8. **A Narrative History of BSD, Dr. Kirk McKusick** — a deep dive into the BSD lineage and its influence on modern operating systems. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ds77e3aO9nA)
9. **The Code: Story of Linux** — the classic documentary by Hannu Puttonen about the creation of the Linux kernel and Linus Torvalds. [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XMm0HsmOTFI)

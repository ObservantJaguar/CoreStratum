# History

The open-source movement is rooted in decades of sharing source code, from the early hacker culture of university laboratories to the free-software ideals of the FSF and the pragmatism of the Open Source Initiative. Understanding this history explains why today's infrastructure is organised the way it is.

## Timeline of open source

### The prehistory: hacker culture (1950sР В Р вЂ Р В РІР‚С™Р Р†Р вЂљРЎС™1970s)

- Software was shipped with hardware and source code was shared freely between researchers and hobbyists.
- The **ARPANET** and university computer labs created the culture of collaboration that later produced GNU and the Internet.

### Unix and the C language (1969Р В Р вЂ Р В РІР‚С™Р Р†Р вЂљРЎС™1970s)

- Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie create **Unix** at Bell Labs.
- Ritchie invents **C** (1972). Unix spreads through academia, and its source code is widely shared and studied — the direct ancestor of today's open systems.

### The GNU Project (1983Р В Р вЂ Р В РІР‚С™Р Р†Р вЂљРЎС™1991)

- **Richard Stallman** announces the GNU Project (Sept 1983), aiming to build a complete free Unix-like operating system.
- Stallman founds the **Free Software Foundation (FSF)** in 1985 and publishes the **GNU General Public License (GPL)** in 1989 — the first strong copyleft licence.
- GNU tools (Emacs, gcc, bash, coreutils) become the standard open toolchain.

### The kernel: Linux (1991)

- **Linus Torvalds** releases the first Linux kernel (0.01) in 1991 on the comp.os.minix newsgroup.
- Combined with GNU tools, Linux forms a complete, free operating system — "GNU/Linux".
- BSD (Berkeley Software Distribution) also continues as a free Unix lineage (FreeBSD, OpenBSD, NetBSD).

### The term "open source" and the OSI (1998)

- Netscape releases Navigator's source code under a free licence (1998) — a turning point.
- **Eric Raymond, Bruce Perens** and others coin the term **"open source"** and co-found the **Open Source Initiative (OSI)**.
- The Debian Free Software Guidelines become the basis of the **Open Source Definition**.

### The dot-com boom and the rise of the web stack (1998Р В Р вЂ Р В РІР‚С™Р Р†Р вЂљРЎС™2005)

- **Apache** becomes the dominant web server; the **LAMP stack** (Linux, Apache, MySQL, PHP) powers much of the early web.
- **Mozilla** is born from Netscape; later Firefox regains browser market share.
- **SourceForge** (1999) popularises hosting; **Git** (2005) and **GitHub** (2008) transform collaboration.
- **Wikipedia** (2001) demonstrates open collaboration at scale.

### The modern era: clouds, containers, open standards (2006Р В Р вЂ Р В РІР‚С™Р Р†Р вЂљРЎС™present)

- **Android** (2008) makes the Linux kernel the world's most widely deployed OS (by devices).
- **Kubernetes** (2014) standardises container orchestration; **Docker** (2013) popularises containers.
- Open clouds (**OpenStack**, **OpenShift**), infrastructure as code (**Terraform**, **Ansible**) and open data standards dominate enterprise tech.
- Open-source licences and business models mature: Red Hat's acquisition, the rise of **open-source companies** such as GitLab, HashiCorp, Confluent.
- The debate over licence "freedom vs sustainability" continues, with moves like **OpenTofu** (fork of Terraform) reflecting community pushback on licence changes.

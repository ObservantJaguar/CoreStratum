# People

The open-source ecosystem is driven by individuals whose ideas and leadership shaped the technology we rely on. This category profiles the key figures behind operating systems, kernels, licensing and major infrastructure projects.

Understanding the motivations of these people — from the idealists who founded the free-software movement to the engineers who maintain modern cloud-native tooling — helps explain the design choices and community values behind the software.

## Founders of the movement

### Richard M. Stallman (RMS)

The founder of the GNU Project and the Free Software Foundation. Stallman developed the free-software philosophy, coined the term "copyleft" and wrote the original General Public License (GPL). His 1983 announcement of the GNU Project ("an operating system that is free software") began the movement that produced a complete free Unix-like system once joined with the Linux kernel. He remains one of the most influential and controversial figures in software history.

**Works:** GNU Project, Emacs, GPL, Free Software Foundation.

### Linus Torvalds

The creator and principal maintainer of the **Linux kernel**, first released in 1991 while he was a student at the University of Helsinki. He modelled Linux on Minix but diverged to create an independent, open kernel that became the foundation of virtually all of today's infrastructure: servers, clouds, Android, embedded systems and the majority of the Internet. He also created **Git** in 2005 to manage kernel development.

**Works:** Linux kernel, Git.

### Eric S. Raymond

A leading thinker of the open-source movement and author of *The Cathedral and the Bazaar* (1997), which articulated the "many eyeballs" (bazaar) model of development. He co-founded the Open Source Initiative (OSI) in 1998 and is a historian and evangelist of hacker culture.

**Works:** *The Cathedral and the Bazaar*, OSI co-founder, fetchmail.

## Systems and infrastructure

### Theo de Raadt

The founder and leader of the **OpenBSD** project and a co-founder of the NetBSD project. Known for his uncompromising code-audit-first approach and the project's slogan "secure by default", he steered OpenBSD to a long record of remote-vulnerability-free default installs.

### Guido van Rossum

The creator of **Python**, one of the most widely used languages in automation, DevOps and data engineering. He served as "Benevolent Dictator for Life" (BDFL) until 2018.

### James Gosling

The creator of the **Java** programming language and virtual machine while at Sun Microsystems, which became the foundation of much enterprise software.

### Bram Moolenaar

The creator and long-time maintainer of the **Vim** text editor, a descendant of Vi, indispensable to admins on remote servers.

## Cloud-native era

### Matt Mullenweg

A co-founder of **WordPress**, which powers a large share of the web, and a strong proponent of open standards and open business models.

### Solomon Hykes

The founder of Docker, Inc. and the creator of **Docker**, which popularised containerisation and triggered the modern container and cloud-native ecosystem.

### Brendan Eich

The creator of **JavaScript**, the language of the web, and a co-founder of Mozilla.

### Mark Shuttleworth

The founder of the **Ubuntu** project and Canonical, which brought Debian-based Linux to mainstream desktops and servers and pioneered a commercial open-source model.

### Aaron Swartz

A programmer and internet activist who helped create RSS and co-founded Reddit; his activism for open access and freedom of information made him an emblematic figure of the open web.
